# Go-Back-N ARQ

> [!abstract] Overview
> **Go-Back-N ARQ (Automatic Repeat reQuest)** is a sliding-window protocol that allows the sender to transmit multiple frames before receiving acknowledgements.
>
> If an acknowledgement is lost or a timeout occurs, the sender uses cumulative ACKs and retransmission rules to recover.

---

## 1. Damaged or Lost ACK

### Case 1 — Next ACK Arrives Before Timer Expires

> [!success] Scenario: Recovery without Retransmission
> The sender receives a later cumulative ACK before the timer expires, even though an earlier ACK was lost.

**Sequence of events**

1. The sender transmits multiple frames without waiting for individual ACKs.
2. An ACK is lost during transmission.
3. A later cumulative ACK arrives before the timer expires.
4. The sender acknowledges the frames covered by that ACK and continues normally.

> [!example] Example
> - Frames `0`, `1`, and `2` are transmitted.
> - An ACK acknowledging an earlier frame is lost.
> - A later cumulative ACK arrives and acknowledges the earlier frames as well.
>
> **Result:** No retransmission is needed for frames already acknowledged.

---

### Case 2 — Next ACK Arrives After Timer Expires

> [!danger] Scenario: Timeout and Retransmission
> If the sender's timer expires before a valid ACK arrives, retransmission begins.

**Sequence of events**

1. The sender transmits multiple frames.
2. The timer runs for the oldest unacknowledged frame.
3. The ACK is lost or delayed beyond the timeout.
4. The timer expires.
5. The sender retransmits the oldest unacknowledged frame and all subsequent outstanding frames.

> [!example] Example
> Suppose the sender transmits Frames `0`, `1`, `2`, and `3`.
>
> If Frame `1` is the oldest unacknowledged frame when the timer expires:
>
> **Retransmitted frames:** `1 → 2 → 3`
>
> Frame `0` does not need retransmission if it has already been acknowledged.

> [!warning] Important
> A delayed ACK may arrive after retransmission has already started. The sender processes acknowledgements according to the protocol's sequence-number rules.

---

## 2. Delayed ACK

> [!info] Definition
> A **Delayed ACK** is an acknowledgement that takes longer than expected to reach the sender. The frame itself may have been received correctly.

### How It Works

1. The sender transmits a frame.
2. The receiver receives the frame correctly.
3. The ACK is delayed in transit or by receiver-side processing.
4. The sender waits for the ACK while its timer is running.
5. If the ACK arrives before timeout, the sender continues normally.
6. If the timer expires first, the sender retransmits the outstanding frames according to Go-Back-N rules.

> [!tip] Remember
> A delayed ACK is not necessarily a lost ACK. It becomes a retransmission problem if the sender times out before receiving a usable acknowledgement.

---

## 3. Quick Revision Table

| Situation | Sender's Action |
|---|---|
| ACK arrives before timeout | Continue normally |
| ACK is lost, but a later cumulative ACK arrives | Earlier frames may be acknowledged |
| ACK arrives after timeout | Retransmission may already have started |
| Timer expires | Retransmit all outstanding frames from the oldest unacknowledged frame |
| ACK is delayed | Wait until ACK arrives or timeout occurs |

---

## 4. Key Points for Exams

> [!note] Must Remember
> - **Cumulative ACK:** Acknowledges frames received successfully and in sequence up to the indicated point.
> - **Timer:** Generally maintained for the oldest unacknowledged frame.
> - **Timeout:** Triggers retransmission of the outstanding frames starting from the oldest unacknowledged frame.
> - **Receiver behaviour:** Accepts frames in order and discards out-of-order frames in standard Go-Back-N.
> - **Lost ACK:** May be recovered through a later cumulative ACK.
> - **Delayed ACK:** Causes retransmission only if a timeout occurs before a usable ACK arrives.

---

## 5. One-Line Memory Trick

> [!quote] Exam Shortcut
> **ACK arrives in time → Continue.**
>
> **Timer expires → Go back to the oldest unacknowledged frame and retransmit forward.**
